# Sam, Meera and Dev: jobs in depth through the question lens

Status: draft for review (2026-11-27). Same lens as Riya (`onboarding-04`):

> **Response = Question intent × Concept shape × Person → lead card**

New lead cards these personas need, in addition to Riya's set (living model, story trail, analogy flip, scale zoom, scenario slider, myth vs fact, versus):

| Lead card | What it is | Mainly for |
|---|---|---|
| **Cause-and-effect chain** | A → B → C with a slider on A ("rates +1% →…") | Sam |
| **Framework card** | A 2×2, a funnel, or a stack: the mental model in one picture | Sam |
| **In-your-world example** | The same concept re-explained with their industry or role | Sam, Dev |
| **One-pager brief** | A summary they can paste into a doc or slide, with sources | Sam |
| **Worked example** | A problem solved step by step, with a reveal at each step | Meera |
| **Derivation walk-through** | The formula built line by line with "why this step" | Meera, Dev |
| **Concept map / prerequisites** | What you need to know first, and what this unlocks | Meera |
| **Exam-style check** | A question in the format of their exam (board, JEE, university) | Meera |
| **Formal model** | Notation, equations, labelled diagram, assumptions stated | Dev |
| **Evidence card** | Key papers and guidelines, strength of evidence, where experts disagree | Dev |
| **Frontier map** | Known / contested / unknown, with what changed recently | Dev |

## Sam, the Builder (34, PM, Austin · also founders and consultants in India)

### Who Sam is when curious
- **Engine:** need. A meeting term, a new project, a podcast, a competitor's move, a decision to make.
- **Triggers:** Slack threads, docs and PRDs, earnings calls, podcasts, LinkedIn posts, a new team or domain.
- **Moments:** 10 minutes before a meeting, the commute, a focused Sunday block. Often multi-day on one topic.
- **Appetite:** deep and practical. 5–15 minutes per session, returns to the same topic over several days.
- **Wants back:** a mental model first, then the key facts, then "so what for me". Trust is everything.
- **Then:** uses it in a meeting, doc or decision; explains it to his team; saves a summary.

### What Sam asks (hypothesis)
| Intent | Typical shape | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **How does X work** (★★★★) | Mechanism (systems, business models) | "How do LLM inference costs work?" · "How does a payments gateway make money?" · "How does a credit score work?" |
| **What is** (★★★) | Abstraction, jargon | "What is a moat, really?" · "What's RAG?" · "What is EBITDA?" |
| **How does X affect Y** (★★★) | Cause and effect | "How do interest rates hit startup valuations?" · "How does churn affect LTV?" |
| **Compare / trade-off** (★★★) | Comparison | "Build vs buy for search?" · "Postgres vs Mongo for our use case?" · "OKRs vs V2MOM?" |
| **What's happening** (★★) | News, trend | "Why is everyone talking about agents?" · "What did the Fed just do?" |
| **What if / should we** (★★) | Scenario, decision | "What if we price per seat vs per usage?" |
| **Why** (★) | Story, history | "Why did WeWork fail?" |

**Pattern:** about 75% is **how it works / what it is / cause-effect / trade-offs**, for **systems and business concepts** in his work context. Little pure-joy curiosity; it's applied curiosity.

### Sam's micro-jobs
| Question type | Micro-job | Whoa | Lead card | Rabbit holes | Ending |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| How × mechanism | When I'm handed a system I don't understand, I want to see its moving parts, so I can reason about it in a meeting | "Now I can draw this on a whiteboard" | **Living model** of the system (flows, money, data) | "Where does it break?" · "Who are the players?" | Sources + **one-pager brief** |
| What is × jargon | When a term keeps coming up, I want a crisp definition plus an example in my world, so I can use it correctly | "Got it, and it applies to us like this" | **In-your-world example** | "Related terms people confuse it with" | Brief + "use it in a sentence" |
| Cause → effect | When a lever moves, I want to see what it moves downstream, so I can anticipate impact | "So *that's* why our numbers move" | **Cause-and-effect chain** with a slider | "Second-order effects" | Brief + "Make it a deep dive" |
| Compare / trade-off | When I must choose, I want the trade-offs laid out for my situation, so I can decide and defend it | "Clear call, with reasons" | **Versus** + **framework card** (2×2) | "When would the other win?" | Decision summary with sources |
| What's happening | When everyone's discussing something, I want the 2-minute why-now, so I'm not caught out | "I'm caught up" | **Why-now card** (timeline + what changed) | "Who wins, who loses" | Brief |
| What if / should we | When I'm weighing options, I want to play out the scenario, so I see the consequences before committing | "That's the risk I missed" | **Scenario slider** | "What would have to be true" | Decision memo |

**Shared jobs:** examples in *his* context (role, industry), visible **sources and confidence**, **resume exactly where he left off**, a **reusable summary**, fits **5-minute gaps**.

### What it changes in onboarding for Sam
- **Sparks:** "How do interest rates hit startups?", "What is RAG, really?", "Build vs buy: how to decide?", "Why is everyone talking about agents?"
- **Personalisation that matters:** his **role and industry**, so examples land in his world. One tap: "I work in: product · engineering · finance · design · founder · other."
- **First Exploration:** the lead card is a cause-and-effect chain, a living system model, or versus. **Sources visible from card 1.** The ending is a one-pager brief + "Make it a deep dive".
- **Wrapped chapter (with import):** "You're a *systems thinker*. 70% of your questions ask how something works or what it affects."
- **Tone:** direct, efficient, no fluff, no confetti.
- **Paywall pitch:** "Go deep on anything for work: deep dives, sources, briefs."

## Meera, the Student (19, engineering, Pune)

### Who Meera is when curious
- **Engine:** being stuck, plus exam pressure, with side curiosity beyond the syllabus.
- **Triggers:** a lecture she didn't follow, a textbook chapter, problem sets, upcoming tests, a YouTube explainer that half-worked, a classmate's question.
- **Moments:** late night after class, weekends before tests, exam season (intense), the bus.
- **Appetite:** deep and graded. 10–30 minutes, needs *proof* she understood, revisits before exams.
- **Wants back:** "I get it" first, then "I can do it" (solve, answer), then "I'll remember it".
- **Then:** solves the problem set, explains it to friends, revises before the exam.

### What Meera asks (hypothesis)
| Intent | Typical shape | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Explain X / what is** (★★★★) | Abstraction | "Explain entropy intuitively" · "What is a Fourier transform?" · "What's an eigenvector, actually?" |
| **How does X work** (★★★) | Mechanism | "How does a transistor work?" · "How does the heart pump?" · "How does recursion work?" |
| **Why (proof, derivation)** (★★★) | Abstraction, derivation | "Why is the derivative of sin cos?" · "Why does E = mc²?" |
| **How to solve** (★★★) | Procedure | "How do I solve this integration by parts?" · "How to balance redox reactions?" |
| **Difference between** (★★) | Comparison | "Mitosis vs meiosis?" · "Speed vs velocity?" · "TCP vs UDP?" |
| **Why (curious)** (★★) | Mechanism, story | "Why do we sleep?" · "Why is the sky blue?" |
| **Is it on the exam / important** (★) | Meta | "What are the most important topics in thermodynamics?" |

**Pattern:** about 70% is **explain / how it works / why-proof / how to solve** on **syllabus concepts**, with comparisons of confusable pairs. The rest is real curiosity she wishes she had time for.

### Meera's micro-jobs
| Question type | Micro-job | Whoa | Lead card | Rabbit holes | Ending |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Explain × abstraction | When a concept is just words to me, I want to *see* it, so it stops being abstract | "Oh, NOW I get it" | **Analogy flip** → **living model** | "What this unlocks next" (concept map) | **Exam-style check** → add to practice |
| How × mechanism | When I can't picture how something works, I want to manipulate it, so I learn by doing | "I made it work myself" | **Living model** (drag, tap, scrub) | "Where it shows up in exams" | Check + practice |
| Why × derivation | When a formula appears from nowhere, I want to build it step by step, so I can reproduce it in the exam | "I could derive this myself" | **Derivation walk-through** | "Common mistakes" | Fill-in-the-step check |
| How to solve | When I'm stuck on a problem type, I want a worked example then my own try, so I can solve the next one alone | "I solved it!" | **Worked example** → a similar problem to try | "Harder variant" | Practice set (3 problems) |
| Difference between | When two things blur together, I want them side by side with the one key difference, so I stop mixing them up | "Never confusing these again" | **Versus** with a memory hook | "Other commonly confused pairs" | Match-pairs check |
| Curious why | When something interesting sidetracks me, I want a quick satisfying answer, so curiosity doesn't cost study time | "That was fun" | Riya-style card (short) | — | Save for later |

**Shared jobs:** *explained differently from class*; **test herself immediately**; **revise in 2 minutes before exams**; **exam-level depth** (board, JEE, university); **real value before paying**.

### What it changes in onboarding for Meera
- **Sparks:** "Explain entropy like I'm 15", "How does a transistor actually work?", "Mitosis vs meiosis: the one difference", "Why do we sleep?"
- **Personalisation that matters:**
  - **Level** (school · college · competitive exam).
  - **Subject** (one tap).
  - **How she likes it explained.** The taste test from Direction 3 is perfect for her.
- **First Exploration:** a living model or analogy flip, then the **exam-style check** (her wow is getting it right), then "Add to practice".
- **Wrapped chapter (with import):** "You're a *figure-it-out* learner. You ask 'explain' and 'why' far more than 'what'."
- **Tone:** encouraging, clear, never condescending.
- **Paywall pitch:** "Understand everything before your exams: unlimited explorations + practice." India-first pricing; the free tier must feel generous.

## Dev, the Specialist (32, senior ML engineer, Bengaluru · also a doctor, a researcher, a hardware engineer)

**V1 persona.** In V0 he shapes the **depth control** and **sources** only.

### Who Dev is when curious
- **Engine:** the frontier. New papers, new tools, adjacent fields, edge cases in his own domain.
- **Triggers:** arXiv and paper threads on X, a new framework release, a production incident, a conference talk, a colleague's question, a clinical case (doctor variant).
- **Moments:** focused deep work, weekend reading, before a design review or a ward round.
- **Appetite:** very deep, precise. 20–60 minutes; wants notation, numbers, references; skips basics.
- **Wants back:** correctness first, then nuance (assumptions, limits, where experts disagree), then speed.
- **Then:** applies it in code or a clinical decision, writes an internal note, debates it with peers.

### What Dev asks (hypothesis)
| Intent | Typical shape | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **How exactly does X work** (★★★★) | Mechanism, formal | "How does speculative decoding work?" · "How does flash attention reduce memory?" · *Doctor:* "Mechanism of SGLT2 inhibitors in heart failure?" |
| **What's new / state of the art** (★★★) | Frontier | "What's the current best approach to long-context retrieval?" · *Doctor:* "Latest guidelines on statins for primary prevention?" |
| **Compare methods** (★★★) | Comparison, formal | "LoRA vs full fine-tuning: trade-offs?" · "RLHF vs DPO?" |
| **Why does X happen (edge case)** (★★) | Mechanism | "Why does my model's loss spike at step 40k?" |
| **What's the evidence** (★★) | Evidence | "Is there evidence that chain-of-thought improves reasoning, or does it just look like it?" |
| **Explain adjacent field** (★★) | Abstraction | An ML engineer asks "How does protein folding prediction work?" |

**Pattern:** **precise mechanism + frontier + evidence + method comparisons.** The basics bore him.

### Dev's micro-jobs
| Question type | Micro-job | Whoa | Lead card | Rabbit holes | Ending |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exact mechanism | When I need to really understand a technique, I want the formal mechanism with assumptions, so I can implement or critique it | "That's the precise picture I needed" | **Formal model** (notation + labelled diagram) + derivation | "Variants", "failure modes" | **Evidence card** + key papers |
| State of the art | When a field moves fast, I want what's current and what changed recently, so I'm not acting on stale knowledge | "Up to date in 10 minutes" | **Frontier map** (known / contested / unknown) | "Open problems" | Reading list |
| Compare methods | When choosing an approach, I want rigorous trade-offs with numbers, so I can justify the choice | "Clear trade-off space" | **Versus** with benchmarks | "When each breaks" | Decision note |
| Evidence | When a claim is popular, I want the strength of evidence, so I don't over-trust hype | "Finally, someone shows the evidence" | **Evidence card** (study quality, effect sizes, disagreement) | "Counter-evidence" | Citations |
| Adjacent field | When I cross into a new field, I want it mapped to concepts I already know, so I learn at expert speed | "Explained in my language" | **In-your-world example** (analogy to *his* field) | "Key papers to read first" | Reading list |

**Shared jobs:** a **depth control** (curious → practitioner → expert); **sources inline**, with dates; **no dumbing down**; **notation when it helps**; flags for **what's uncertain**.

### What it changes in the product (V1) and onboarding (light, V0)
- **V0:** onboarding offers "How deep?": *curious · know some · expert*. Expert unlocks formal and evidence cards and sources on every card. The Builder and some Students benefit too.
- **V1:** a "Specialist mode" (the dark glass card in the design system) with frontier maps, paper ingestion (paste an arXiv link or PDF), and field-specific vocab.
- **Sparks (expert):** "How does speculative decoding work?", "RLHF vs DPO: what actually differs?", "What's the evidence chain-of-thought helps?"
- **Wrapped chapter (with import):** "You live at the frontier. 40% of your questions are about things published this year."
- **Tone:** precise, dense, no exclamation marks.
- **Paywall pitch:** "Expert depth with sources, on anything."
